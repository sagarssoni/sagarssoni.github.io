About

Building AI people can trust

I'm a tinkerer at heart who builds production AI for high-stakes environments. A good model is just the start: latency, reliability, interpretability, data quality, observability and regulatory evidence decide whether it becomes a product people trust.

Over 10+ years I've worked across computer vision and AI, and I'm now exploring the limits of state-of-the-art AI in healthcare and beyond. I've contributed to three FDA-cleared products, including the world's first FDA-cleared OCT angiography metrics, and co-authored patents on automated clinical imaging and AI-driven acquisition.

I work where hardware meets AI. Knowing the sensors and systems a model runs on means I see the whole product, not just the algorithm.

I lead architecture and roadmaps across firmware, hardware, software and AI, and take systems end to end: from data strategy and research through deployment, clinical validation, post-market monitoring and regulatory submission. That includes the unglamorous parts that make AI hold up in the field, like device observability, fleet monitoring and release engineering.

I help companies build AI-native engineering teams, with agentic development workflows, shared engineering guidelines and knowledge bases that AI tools can actually use, so a small team moves at the pace of a much larger one.

Where AI in health is going

The bets I'd make for a team building in health AI today.

Safety evals for clinical AI

Evals that test whether a model knows the limits of what it knows and hands a case to a human when it should. They belong at the start of a product, not the end.

EvalsCalibrationEscalationAlignment

Interpretability and explainability

Looking inside models to understand what they've actually learned, from attention and attribution maps to sparse autoencoders and circuits. In health, models have to be right for the right reasons, and teams need to be able to show that to clinicians and regulators.

Mechanistic interpretabilitySparse autoencodersFeature attributionExplainable clinical AI

Agentic systems in health

Agents that take on real clinical workflows like triage, documentation and follow-up, with humans in the loop where the stakes are high.

Tool useHuman-in-the-loopClinical workflows

From early detection to precision longevity

The same signals that catch disease early, from retinal images to wearables and labs, can also track how a person is aging. Models that fuse them could shift care from treating disease to measuring biological age and intervening years earlier, tuned to each person.

Work I've shipped

OCT · Real time

Live eye tracking for retinal OCT

Tracking that follows small eye movements during a scan and keeps it on target, cutting motion artifacts and the need for repeat scans.

OCT angiography · FDA-cleared

First FDA-cleared OCT angiography metrics

Quantitative measures of retinal blood flow, such as vessel density and areas of non-perfusion, so clinicians can track diabetic retinopathy, wet AMD and glaucoma with numbers instead of judging images by eye.

OCT · Volumetric

3D retinal layer segmentation

Algorithms that trace each retinal layer through a full 3D scan. They underpin thickness maps and the depth slices used to read blood flow.

Retinal imaging · Zero to one

Handheld retinal camera

A portable camera that images the retina without dilating drops, taken from first prototype to FDA clearance so primary care clinics can screen patients without a specialist visit.

Representation learning

Few-shot and class-incremental learning

Deep metric learning systems that recognize new classes from a handful of examples and keep learning without forgetting what they already know.

Engineering · Agentic

Agentic development workflows

Company-wide rollout of agent-assisted engineering, with shared guidelines and knowledge bases AI tools can read, so agents work inside the same rules as the team.
